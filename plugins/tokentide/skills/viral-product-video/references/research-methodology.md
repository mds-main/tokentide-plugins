# Deep product & brand research → virality strategy

This is the most important phase. Generation is cheap to redo; a wrong angle wastes every video
built on it. The goal is not a report for its own sake but three things the creative work needs:
**what to show** (hero moments), **what to say, in whose words** (VOC language), and **why someone
would send it to a friend** (shareability). Everything below serves those three.

## 0. Principles and evidence rules
- **Evidence first.** Every factual claim in the dossier gets a source tag `[n]` with URL and access
  date. Write `UNKNOWN` instead of guessing; mark interpretations as *(inference)*. Guessed facts
  turn into false claims in videos, which is both a legal and a trust problem.
- **Customer words beat marketing words.** Collect quotes verbatim. Hooks written in the exact
  language customers use outperform polished copy because viewers recognise themselves.
- **Recency.** Platform trends and competitor content older than ~90 days are context, not signal.
  Note dates on everything trend-related.
- **Triangulate.** A pain mentioned once is an anecdote; across 3+ sources it is a pattern. Rank
  clusters by frequency × intensity.
- **Respect boundaries.** Use public pages. Don't scrape behind logins, don't collect personal data
  about reviewers (keep site + date + rating, not names), and don't use a browser on logged-in
  platforms unless the user explicitly allows it.
- **Budget the effort.** Full campaign: 25–60 searches + fetches, ≥25 distinct sources. Quick test
  mode: ~10 searches, 15 quotes, label everything as provisional.

## 1. Phase 1 — Product & brand intelligence
Fill `research/01-product-brand-dossier.md`.

**Sources, in order:** the product page (PDP) and pricing → home/about/mission → FAQ, docs, manuals,
spec sheets → press kit, press coverage, founder interviews/podcasts → marketplace listings (Amazon,
retailers, app stores: also the Q&A section) → review aggregators → the brand's existing social
accounts → job posts (reveal strategy and markets) → trademark/regulatory listings when relevant.

**Extract:**
1. *Positioning* (April Dunford's components): competitive alternatives incl. "do nothing", unique
   attributes, the value they enable, best-fit customers, market category. Write the one-line
   positioning statement last.
2. *Feature → benefit → emotional outcome ladder.* For each feature ask "so what?" twice. Add the
   column "what we can SHOW on camera" — this is where hero moments come from.
3. *Proof inventory*: numbers, tests, certifications, awards, press, rating + review count. Note the
   exact wording allowed; anything not provable cannot appear as a claim in a video.
4. *Offer & commerce*: price, guarantee, channels, promos, TikTok Shop / Instagram shopping status.
5. *Voice & visual identity*: 3 voice adjectives, we-say/never-say, hex colors, fonts, logo files,
   photo style. If no guidelines exist, infer them from the site and label *(inference)*.
6. *Product bible* (feeds every prompt): canonical descriptor ≤25 words, exact on-pack text,
   never-change list, hero angles, hard-to-render details, available real photos. Ask the user for
   high-resolution photos from several angles on plain backgrounds if they don't exist — product
   fidelity depends on them more than on any model choice.
7. *Social audit*: per platform, handle, followers, cadence, median views of the last ~30 posts,
   top 5 posts with format and hook, comment themes, gaps. If the brand has no presence, audit the
   founder's or the closest analogous brand's.

## 2. Phase 2 — Audience & voice of customer
Fill `research/02-audience-voc.md`.

**Where to mine (pick what fits the product type):**
| Product type | Best VOC sources |
|---|---|
| Physical / DTC | Amazon & retailer reviews (3★ = nuanced, 1–2★ = objections & competitor pains, 5★ = desires & "aha"), Trustpilot, Reddit threads, YouTube review comments, TikTok/IG comments |
| App / SaaS | App Store & Google Play reviews, G2, Capterra, Product Hunt, Reddit, X replies, Hacker News |
| Service / local | Google reviews, Yelp, Tripadvisor, forums, Facebook groups (public) |
| Any | **Competitors' reviews** — their 1–3★ reviews are your pain list and your positioning gap |

**Protocol:**
1. Collect ≥50 verbatim quotes from ≥4 source types (quick mode: 15). Record source, rating, date.
2. Tag each: pain · desire · objection · trigger moment · outcome · language. Rate intensity 1–3
   (3 = strong emotion, swearing, ALL CAPS, "life-changing", "never again").
3. Cluster and rank by frequency × intensity: top 7 pains, desires, objections; trigger moments;
   "aha" moments.
4. Build the language bank: phrases to reuse in hooks, category words customers use vs. jargon they
   never use, metaphors and before/after descriptions.
5. Define 2–4 segments with a JTBD statement each ("When ___, I want to ___, so I can ___").
6. Set **awareness level** (Schwartz) and **market sophistication** per segment:

| Awareness | What they know | Hook strategy that fits |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Not the problem | Story, curiosity, pattern interrupt, entertainment; product appears late |
| Problem-aware | The pain, not solutions | Name the pain in their words (PAS), relatable POV |
| Solution-aware | Solutions exist | Mechanism ("why this works"), comparison vs. the old way, demo |
| Product-aware | Your product | Proof, objections answered, social proof (real), unboxing/try-on |
| Most aware | Ready to buy | Offer, urgency, bundle, new variant |

Sophistication: in crowded categories (level 4–5) plain claims are ignored; lead with a new
mechanism, a surprising demo, or identity/community instead of "the best X".

## 3. Phase 3 — Competitors, ads & trends
Fill `research/03-competitors-trends.md`.

1. **Competitive set (5–10):** direct, indirect, substitutes, aspirational brands, and *niche
   creators* (often the real competition for attention).
2. **Content teardown:** for each, the top posts (≥5) by views on each platform you can see. Record
   the first line and first visual of the hook verbatim, the format, the length, the CTA and the
   production style. Flag posts beating the account's median by ≥3× — those formats are proven.
   Platforms often block automated fetching; use web search with `site:tiktok.com` / `site:instagram.com`
   operators, public profile pages, YouTube Shorts as a proxy, and ask the user for screenshots or
   exports when you hit a wall.
3. **Ad intelligence:** Meta Ad Library (filter country + active; ads running ≥30 days are likely
   profitable → validated angles), TikTok Creative Center (Top Ads by industry/objective, Keyword
   Insights, trending hashtags/songs/creators), TikTok Ad Library (EU), Google Ads Transparency
   Center, LinkedIn ad library for B2B. Capture ≥10 ads.
4. **Trends & search:** TikTok and Instagram search suggestions ("others searched for"), Google
   autosuggest and Google Trends (seasonality, rising related queries), X Explore. Note which sounds
   are usable by business accounts (TikTok Commercial Music Library) — trending commercial tracks
   are often restricted for brands.
5. **Cultural calendar:** next 8 weeks of holidays, seasons, events, category days and platform
   moments relevant to the product and market.
6. **White space:** formats that work in adjacent niches that no competitor uses; things competitors
   do that you should *not* copy (and why — often fake UGC, misleading claims or saturated memes).

## 4. Phase 4 — Virality strategy
Fill `strategy/04-virality-strategy.md`. This is synthesis: do it yourself, not in a subagent.

**4.1 Hero moments.** List every visual moment the product creates — transformation, reveal,
satisfying action (pour, click, snap, peel), before/after, speed, durability/torture test,
unexpected use, scale surprise, sound (ASMR), texture, the "aha" moment from VOC. Score each 0–3 on:
visual demonstrability, emotional payoff, novelty/surprise, shareability, product-centrality (the
product causes the payoff, not just appears), feasibility (with real footage + AI). Rank by total.

**4.2 Angles (≥10).** Use these generators, each grounded in a VOC quote:
pain relief · desire fulfilment · identity ("for people who…") · enemy (the old way, the status quo,
never a named competitor without substantiation) · myth-bust · mechanism ("why it works") ·
comparison vs. the old way · real social proof · value/price logic · seasonal/trend tie-in ·
behind-the-scenes/founder · absurd humour · education/how-to · challenge/test.

**4.3 Hook bank (≥30).** Combine hook templates (references/hooks-formats.md) with VOC phrases.
Each hook = on-screen text ≤8 words + spoken line ≤12 words + first visual (0–1 s). Tag angle,
awareness level, platform fit and the quote it came from.

**4.4 Shareability filter.** Score each concept on STEPPS (Berger), 0–2 each: Social currency,
Triggers, Emotion (high arousal: awe, amusement, surprise, anger/anxiety beat sadness/contentment),
Public, Practical value, Stories. Greenlight at ≥7/12 *and* a completed send test:
"[persona] sends this to [person] because [reason]". If you can't finish that sentence, it won't spread.

**4.5 Pillars, series, mix.** 3–5 pillars with a job each (reach / trust / convert / community).
A sensible start: 40% value/education, 25% entertainment/trend, 20% demo/proof, 15% community/offer.
Design ≥2 recurring series (named, with an episode template and a recurring element such as a
mascot, title card, sound or presenter). Series are what turn viewers into followers.

**4.6 Platform adaptation.** Same core idea, different cut: TikTok favours native lo-fi, search
keywords and trend participation; Instagram rewards sends, aesthetics and originality (Trial Reels
for tests); X rewards topical takes, conversation and replies. See references/platforms.md.

**4.7 Calendar & tests.** Two-week calendar (platform × slot × video × hook variant). Test one
variable at a time, hooks first. Define KPIs and decision thresholds (references/growth-loop.md).

**4.8 Risk register.** Claims needing proof, regulated-category rules, cultural sensitivities,
AI-backlash risk (mitigate with honesty, labeling, product-centric creativity), IP and likeness.

## 5. Search query bank
Replace {brand}, {product}, {category}, {competitor}, {pain}, {market}.
- `{brand} {product}` · `{brand} review` · `{product} vs` · `{product} worth it` · `{brand} founder interview`
- `"{product}" site:reddit.com` · `{category} recommendations site:reddit.com` · `"{competitor}" problem OR issue OR "stopped working"`
- `{competitor} review "after * months"` · `{category} "I wish" OR "why doesn't"`
- `site:amazon.com {category} best seller` (then read 3★ and 1★ reviews of the top 3)
- `site:tiktok.com {category}` · `site:instagram.com {category} reel` · `{category} tiktok viral`
- `{category} ads examples {year}` · `TikTok Creative Center {category} top ads`
- `{category} trends {year}` · `{category} statistics {year}` · `{pain} {market}`
- App/SaaS: `{product} site:g2.com` · `{product} alternatives` · `{product} app store reviews`
Search in the target market's language too; VOC from the actual market beats translated VOC.

## 6. When information is thin
New products, pre-launch, or tiny brands have few reviews. Then:
- Research the **category** and the **closest analogues** (competitors' reviews become your VOC).
- Ask the user for internal material: customer emails, support tickets, survey answers, sales-call
  notes, beta feedback, founder story, product test data.
- Interview mode: ask the user 5–8 sharp questions (who buys, why now, objections heard, the moment
  customers "get it", what they say to friends).
- Run in *hypothesis mode*: label every unvalidated assumption and plan the first batch as tests
  that validate or kill them (growth-loop.md).

## 7. Parallel research with subagents
If an agent/subagent tool is available, run Phases 1–3 in parallel — one agent per template — and
synthesize Phase 4 yourself. Give each agent: the product name/URL, market and language, the path
to its template, the relevant section of this file, the evidence rules (section 0) and the minimum
bars from section 8. Ask for the filled template plus a list of sources. Afterwards, review for
contradictions and gaps before synthesizing. Without subagents, do the phases sequentially.

## 8. Research QA checklist and the approval gate
- [ ] Dossier: all sections filled or UNKNOWN; ≥10 sources; product bible complete; real photos listed.
- [ ] VOC: ≥50 verbatim quotes, ≥4 source types; 7 pains / 7 desires / 7 objections; segments with
      JTBD and awareness level; language bank.
- [ ] Competitors: 5–10 accounts; ≥5 top posts each with verbatim hooks; ≥10 ads; trends dated.
- [ ] Strategy: ranked hero moments; ≥10 angles; ≥30 hooks tied to quotes; 3–5 pillars; ≥2 series;
      first batch with STEPPS ≥7 and a send test; calendar; KPIs; risk register.
- [ ] Every claim planned for videos exists in the proof inventory.

**Gate:** before paid generation, show the user one screen: positioning line, top 3 angles, top 10
hooks, the series ideas, the first 3–6 concepts with estimated cost — and get a go-ahead (unless
they already said to proceed autonomously). Deliver the research and strategy as documents they
can keep (the environment's document/artifact facilities if present, otherwise the markdown files).
